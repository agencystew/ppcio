# Stage 4: build the page

Work in the work folder (landing-pages/<site>/). Every path below is in it, except KIT paths.

Your goal: take this business's landing page and make it world class, in their own brand, using all the creative quirks that make it theirs, so it turns more of the people who click their ads into customers than any page in their market. Be insanely ambitious. This is the best work of your career: a page the owner opens, says wow, sees their own brand at its best, and puts live today. The argument, the copy, the layout, the art direction and the motion are yours to decide.

The audit (audit.md) is settled. Do not score their live page again: inputs/live-page-today.png and inputs/live-phone-today.png show it, craft.md has measured how it is built, and live-parts.json lists what it already does (its form and where it sits, its FAQ, its logo row, its reviews). Its strongest parts are your material: keep each one or beat it (keep.md, step 1). Go through their real photos, their audit and their reviews. Work out what this page is for, and design for that. A hot tub maker shows its real tubs, big, so a buyer wants one.

A page that informs is not a page that sells. A buyer who lands here must feel the problem they came to fix, see what they get when it is fixed, trust that this business gets them there, see the offer and what it removes (time, effort, risk), have their doubts answered, and know the one thing to do now: with a form when their site takes enquiries. There is no fixed list of sections, but that case is made in that order (case.md, step 1).

The first screen shows, in this order: a headline that carries the outcome the buyer wants or the offer itself, in the owner's or their customers' words; the words they searched for, in the headline or the line under it, so they know in one second they are in the right place; their strongest real review or figure beside the first button; and the form or the call, where their live page has it on its first screen. A headline that only names the category ("Meeting room AV for Sydney offices") is a fault. When their live headline already says an outcome, keep it word for word unless you have a stronger true line. Prices and specs go in the hero only when their live page leads with them. Every fact about a product, a price, an offer or a place comes from their own site or the owner, exactly as they state it: a tub their page lists as Ø205 is round.

Their brand is your starting point, never something to replace: their colours, type, logo and voice, made better. Their real photos are the page's imagery: lead the hero with their best one, big. This holds when the chosen style has a text-only first screen: take the style's type, spacing and mood, and still show their best photo on the first screen.

You write the page. Every headline, button and line of copy is yours, written in the owner's voice from their own words. Only quotes, figures, awards and claims are taken word for word. A sentence from their site goes on the page only when a sharp marketer would have written it; their filler never becomes a headline.

Read first: KIT/TASTE.md, the standard every page is judged against. Then your materials, several in one turn:
- inputs/live-page-today.png, inputs/live-phone-today.png and inputs/live-full.jpg: their page today. inputs/site-page-text.md: its text. inputs/pages/*.md: the text of their other pages (projects, about, services), for facts, proof and photos the landing page lacks. inputs/site-facts.json: its forms (what each asks and its button), buttons, phone links and tags. audit.md: the audit and what the owner chose.
- facts.md: every real customer quote word for word with its name, their real figures, offers and phone, the problems their buyers have and the outcomes they get, the buyer's doubts in order, and what winning pages in this kind of business do.
- live-parts.json (when present): what their live page does, measured in a real browser after scrolling: whether its form is on the first laptop and phone screen, how many FAQ questions it answers, whether it shows a row of partner or client logos, and the reviews, ratings and review counts a visitor sees (widgets included).
- craft.md: their live page's type, colours, buttons, shapes and motion, measured, with the URLs of their font files.
- inputs/photo-sheet.jpg (open it first) and inputs/photos.md: their real photos; inputs/photos/NN.jpg to look closer. Some are partners or awards, not theirs.
- directions/choice.md and the chosen direction's first screen (directions/<x>.html): the owner picked this. Build the page from it.
- KIT/library/refs/flock-1.jpg, flock-2.jpg, flock-3.jpg: the LEVEL of finish we hold you to. Flock is another business with its own voice and look. Take its standard (care in every detail, real photos used big and well, depth and layering, a signature moment, colour used with confidence), never its typefaces, its italic accents, its palette or its sentence style.
- KIT/library/refs/our-best-*.jpg: pages from this studio the owner of the kit backed, for other businesses. They are the bar: match their finish, then beat it. Never take their layout, palette or words.
- The library: `bash KIT/scripts/kit.sh library niche <slug>` (the best pages in their kind of business), `library find <words>` (pages from other trades) and `library sections <words>` (the best single sections, each with the move that makes it work). The idea that sets this page apart often comes from another industry. Take the move, never the look, words or photos.
- KIT/library/refs/category-*.jpg: pages rated best in class, from other trades. Study how each makes the offer clear in one look, puts proof beside the button and brings the action back where a buyer decides.

How you work:
0. The audit's top 3 fixes are the brief. Every one of them is done in the new page, word for word where the audit gives the words. When a fix needs a fact they have not given (what the warranty covers, the visit fee, what an offer includes), build the best version with what exists, and ask for the fact in ONE line in the page-ready message. An offer with an end date gets that date on the page.
1. The buyer first. Write buyer.md: who buys this (their job and the moment they search), what they must know to act, in the order they need it, and which facts are only filters to answer in one line (where you work, opening hours). Start from the buyer's questions in facts.md and audit.md, in their order. Add only what is missing. No question is answered twice on the page. Where their site shows no price, plan how the page explains pricing and exactly what to send for a quote; where it has no reviews, list the proof that does exist (accreditations, years, named fleet or team, project photos) and write down the gap.
   Then write keep.md: what their live page already does well, from inputs/site-page-text.md, the live screenshot and live-parts.json. List its five strongest lines (headline, promise, the problems it names), its form and where it sits, its proof (review count and rating, named clients, logos, figures) and its FAQ. Each one appears on the new page, or keep.md names the stronger part that replaces it and why.
   Then write case.md, the case the page makes, in six short parts, each from facts.md or site-page-text.md only:
   1. The problem: what goes wrong for this buyer today, in their words (facts.md problems, their reviews, the live page). When there is none, write that, and the page leads with the outcome and the offer.
   2. The outcome: what they get when it is fixed, in the owner's or their customers' words.
   3. Why this business: the two or three strongest proofs, taken from the top of facts.md "## Proof". A named review or a rating with its count sits in the first screen or right beside the main action. Every item in "## Proof" appears on the page, or plan.md says why it is weaker than what replaced it.
   4. The offer: what the buyer gets now, on what terms, and what it takes away: waiting, effort or risk.
   5. The doubts: the buyer's questions from buyer.md, in order.
   6. The action: the one thing to do, and what happens next.
2. Brand. Write brand.md. Write these parts:
   - Their colours: which logo is theirs (look at it in the screenshot and photos), its colours from craft.md and the screenshot, and which colour leads their brand. Their brand colours carry the big fields and the buttons; anything you add is a quiet neutral or a tint of their own colours.
   - Their type: describe their logo's letterforms (geometric, rounded, slab, script, condensed, serif, hand-drawn) and choose a pairing that belongs to THIS business and its category, with the reason. When craft.md shows real fonts of their own (not a theme default such as Open Sans or Arial, or a site builder's stock font), use them the way craft.md says (a Google Fonts link, or inputs/fonts.css pasted into the `<style>`; never their own font URLs, which their server blocks), and keep their accent style; keep a strong site's button style and motion too. Headlines are set in normal sentence case. A serif with italic accent words is the look the owner said grates; earn anything near it.
   - Their voice: copy 12 phrases in their own words from site-page-text.md and 6 from their customers' reviews in facts.md. Headlines and copy are built from these words and this way of talking: plain, specific, the way this owner speaks to a customer. No slogans (a line with no fact, no outcome and no buyer problem in it), no paired fragments, no groups of three, no lines an owner would never say out loud. Every headline carries at least one of: the outcome the buyer wants, a specific fact (a number, a name, a time), or the buyer's problem in their words. A promise the business makes on its own site may be used in its own words. Each section heading says what the buyer gets, or the answer itself, the way a buyer would say it, never only the topic: "We measure the room first, so the price holds", never "What the free assessment covers"; "$0 down, and every fee shown before you sign", never "Every fee on both leases". Give the buyer everything they need, cut into small pieces: headlines of about six to ten words, sentences of about thirteen, paragraphs of one to three short sentences, and a first screen under about eighty words.
   - Their loudness: quiet, confident or loud, judged from their live page, logo and photos, with the reason. It sets how far the signature moment may travel down the page.
   - Their actions: the two or three real things a buyer can do here (call, book, ask for a quote), what each one does, and one name for each that never changes. The filled main button is the way their buyers act: for trades and emergencies that is the phone call, big; for booked services and software it is the booking or the form. Under the main button, one short line says what happens next. The main button is on the first phone screen. On a phone, a slim bar keeps the main action in reach once the hero has scrolled away. The same action, with the same name, comes back where a buyer decides: after the proof, after the cost and at the end.
3. The signature moment: stage 3 built the first screen in the chosen style, or picked one of three directions (directions/choice.md). Use it, with what they asked to mix in. How far the moment travels follows their loudness: a quiet brand keeps it to the hero, the buttons and one more section; a loud brand may carry it down the page, as long as every part a buyer needs is still there and easy to read.
4. Write plan.md. First the art direction (the pairing and colours from brand.md, how photos and interface layer, the chosen moment). Then name one working moment a buyer would use, made only from their real content (a before-and-after slider, a calculator from their real terms, a picker, a coverage checker), or write that their facts do not support one. It must work on a phone. Use that moment once, in one section: its heading says what it does for the buyer ("Every job we do, priced"), never the name of the device ("Tick off your list"), and one line under its action says what happens next, from facts.md. When their page sells three or more services, the page serves the whole range: the hero names at least three of them by their own names, and no section is built around one service alone. The owner's note steers the plan; its words are never printed as a heading or a theme. Then one table of every section, in order from the top of the page:
   | Section | Job | Buyer question | Proof | From |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   Section is its short name. Job is its part of case.md: problem, outcome, proof, offer, doubt or action. The order follows case.md: the problem (when there is one) comes before the doubts, and real proof sits on the first or second screen, never halfway down. Doubts may share a section, and small ones may close the page as a short FAQ. Buyer question is the one question it answers, in the buyer's words. Proof is the real fact, quote or photo it shows. From names the page its move comes from: their page, a competitor by its name, or a library page by its domain, such as attio.com. Each buyer question in facts.md is answered somewhere: in its own doubt row, in a row it shares, or in the closing FAQ. Take two to four moves from the library and name each one in From, or say under the table why none fits. Under the table, give the hero and each section its layout and five headings in the owner's voice, and pick the one that tells the buyer most clearly what they get, with the reason.
   The offer comes first. Under the table, write the one offer line for the first screen: what the buyer gets and on what terms, made only of facts the business states in facts.md or site-page-text.md, word for word where it is a price, a time or a promise (a plumber that states "Fixed price before we start" and "2-hour arrival slot" leads with those). The section that answers what happens after they call or send sits no lower than third. When their facts hold no offer, write that, and lead with the strongest real proof instead. Never make one up: no "free", price, guarantee or time their own words do not state.
   Then test the case before you build. Start a subagent (the Agent tool) as an outside conversion lead, so the check has fresh eyes. Tell it to write its reply to case-check.md in one Write call, and in the same message run `kit.sh await landing-pages/<site> case-check.md`, so you wait in a tool call. Write nothing in chat while it works. Give it the prompt in KIT/steps/4-review.md, part "The case check prompt", with the paths of inputs/site-page-text.md, facts.md, case.md and plan.md. Fix plan.md and case.md for every point you agree with, and write under the table what you changed, then the line `Case check: READY`. The structure can still change now; after page.html it cannot.
5. Write page.html: one self-contained file, their photo URLs, Google Fonts welcome. It is the page, not a draft: the review rounds only fix faults (never a new layout), so finish it now: perfect the type, spacing, rhythm, imagery and motion at both widths, push the signature moment as far as their loudness allows, and leave out the marks of generic AI design (eyebrow tags, numbered sections, code-style text, icon-card grids, glows, frosted glass and the rest): the guard (KIT/steps/4-review.md) names each one it finds as an ai mark, with its fix. Keep their live page's form, if it has one, asking the same things, and where it sits: a form on their first screen stays on your first screen. Keep every link pointing at a part of this page, except their real booking, scheduling or checkout page and their phone number: those keep their live address. Never paste their tracking tags (inputs/tracking-tags.html) into page.html: every render would send a visit to their real analytics and ads. Put this one line in the `<head>` where they belong: `<!-- ppcio:tracking-tags -->`. At go-live, pack writes the live copy with their tags in that place.
6. Review and finish: follow KIT/steps/4-review.md.


What is fixed:
- The phone number: when their live page shows more than one, use the number in their live page's first screen and do not stop to ask. In the page-ready message, add one line: which number the page uses, and that a call tracking number can replace it.
- A rating with no count and no source ("5-Star Rated") stays off the page. Show a rating only with its count and where it comes from (Google, Yelp).
- Every quote, figure, award and claim comes from facts.md, site-page-text.md or their photos, word for word. Invent nothing.
- Body text is 17px or more and nothing is under 14px. Buttons look like buttons and say what happens next. Every control works, or it becomes a picture or a sentence. Nothing pretends to send: a form that posts to their own form tool keeps that `action`; any other form gets `action="#"`, and on submit it shows "This form is not connected yet" until stage 5 connects it.
- Their photos (their own URLs) run at their own shape, never stretched; a photo with text baked into it is never a big image.
- No made images exist here. Where no photo fits, the moment is built from type, colour and interface.
