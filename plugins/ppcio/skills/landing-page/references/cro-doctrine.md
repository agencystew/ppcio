# Paid-Search CRO Doctrine

What makes a landing page actually convert paid clicks: distilled from a decade of paid-search marketing and thousands of real audits. Read this when diagnosing a page, planning a section order, or deciding what moves conversions most.

## 1. Paid Search Is Not Organic Browsing

A visitor from Google Ads is not reading your brand story. They typed a specific problem or buying query, clicked a paid ad that promised a specific answer, and will leave in 5 seconds if the first screen does not prove they landed in the right place.

When rules pull in different directions, settle them in this exact priority order:

1. **The search answered first**: headline + subhead confirm the exact service/product and outcome the buyer searched for.
2. **The offer clear**: what they get, on what terms, and what risk or waiting it removes.
3. **Trust at a glance**: real reviews with counts and sources, real photos of the work/team, credentials right beside the action.
4. **Clear in 5 seconds**: plain language a cold stranger understands on a phone without decoding clever headlines.
5. **Proof beside every ask**: reviews, numbers, or risk-reversal placed directly next to every button and form, never stranded in a separate section halfway down.
6. **Fewest possible steps**: no unnecessary clicks, extra nav escapes, or form fields that have not earned their place.

**A page that informs is not a page that sells.** Every section exists to move a sceptical buyer one step closer to enquiring or buying.

## 2. Schwartz's 5 Stages of Buyer Awareness

Paid clicks arrive at a specific point on Eugene Schwartz's awareness ladder. Matching the page's opening to the buyer's stage is one of the highest-leverage CRO moves:

| Stage | What the buyer typed | What the opening screen MUST do | Classic mistake |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Unaware** | Rarely paid search (mostly cold social/display) | Lead with a vivid symptom or story | Pitching a quote form to someone who does not know they have a problem |
| **Problem-aware** | *"roof leaking after hail"*, *"wasted google ads spend"* | Name the problem plainly, show the relief/outcome, prove you fix it | Leading with company history or a bare price with zero outcome framing |
| **Solution-aware** | *"metal roof replacement"*, *"emergency plumber austin"* | Show why YOUR version beats alternatives, stack proof, make the next step easy | Generic category label (*"Austin Plumbing Services"*) that could be any of 50 competitors |
| **Product-aware** | *"Erie Home metal roof reviews"*, *"Brand X pricing"* | Lead with differentiation, real reviews, pricing/financing, and guarantee | Explaining basic category concepts they already know while burying price/terms |
| **Most-aware** | *"Brand X free quote"*, retargeting clicks | Put the offer, proof, and action right at the top with zero friction | Making them scroll past 6 educational sections to find the form or phone |

**Diagnosis rule:** When traffic is *more* aware than the page (negative gap, e.g., high-intent *"roof replacement quote"* landing on a multi-division corporate homepage), conversions bleed because buyers cannot act fast. When the page assumes *more* awareness than the visitor has (positive gap, e.g., pitching *"Book a Demo"* with zero explanation of the problem fixed), cold clicks bounce.

## 3. The Value Equation (Under the Hood)

Every strong landing page quietly maximizes four levers: **never print these labels in customer-facing copy**, only use them to judge and sharpen the page:

1. **Dream outcome**: the specific end-state the buyer wants (*"Your Self Assessment, filed by a real UK accountant"* beats *"You answer. An accountant files."*: lead with relief, not chores).
2. **Perceived likelihood of success**: stacked, verifiable proof (real photos, named customer quotes, review count + source, credentials) + risk-reversal right next to the CTA. Proof outsells promises every time.
3. **Time delay**: how fast they hear back, get a visit, or see the outcome (*"2-hour arrival window"*, *"Estimate in 24 hours"*: only when true).
4. **Effort & sacrifice**: what the buyer does *not* have to do (*"No excavation"*, *"3-step process"*, *"We handle the insurance paperwork"*). Keep "How it works" to 3–4 steps; 5+ reads as work.

## 4. Five Depths of Trust

Countless pages claim they "have trust" because they dropped 4 grey logos in a strip. Real conversion trust operates across 5 distinct depths:

1. **Identity**: real business name, physical location/service area, real team/crew/vans/practitioner photos (never stock handshakes), named owner or lead.
2. **Volume**: aggregate rating *with* count and verifiable source (*"4.8 from 40,000+ reviews on Google & BBB"*, never bare *"5-Star Rated"*).
3. **Quality**: specific, named customer reviews quoting the exact objection or outcome the buyer cares about, placed right where they decide.
4. **Association**: trade licences, insurance, accreditations, certifications, real manufacturer or client logos (with exact licence numbers where regulated).
5. **Risk-reversal**: warranty, satisfaction guarantee, fixed upfront price, free cancellation, or zero-obligation visit terms placed beside the primary button.

## 5. Buyer-Arc Page Anatomy

Section count is not the metric: buyer fit is. Fast-decision ecommerce may convert in 4 sections; high-ticket considered services may need 8–10. Whatever the length, the argument runs in this order:

1. **First screen (Outcome + Proof + Action)**: confirm the search, state the outcome/offer, show the strongest proof, present the main action.
2. **Problem or Self-Recognition**: show you understand the exact mess or risk they came to solve (skip if most-aware / brand traffic).
3. **What You Get / How It Works (≤3–4 steps)**: tangible outcomes, real photos of the work/product, and what happens right after they click or call (no lower than section 3).
4. **Proof & Real Examples**: named reviews, before-and-afters, local jobs, case results.
5. **Offer & Risk-Reversal**: what is included, pricing/financing framing, guarantee, what removes fear.
6. **Objection-Clearing FAQ**: only the real doubts that stop a buyer from acting (price, timing, disruption, suitability, warranty): never filler.
7. **Final Action Band**: repeat the exact same primary CTA label + proof + what happens next.

## 6. Page Archetypes & What Changes

Never grade every page with one rigid template:

- **Dedicated Paid-Search LP:** Full conversion discipline: zero competing nav links, tight message match, single primary action, all 7 offer checks apply.
- **Brand Homepage (receiving paid traffic):** Usually leaks by splitting attention across multiple divisions/services. Either build a dedicated LP for the ad group or tighten the hero so the paid service, proof, and action are unmistakable above the fold.
- **Lead-Gen / Quote Funnel:** Price often varies by job, so do not force a fake fixed price: judge *pricing transparency* (starting range, what affects cost, free written quote before work starts, financing terms).
- **E-commerce PDP / Category Page:** Variant selection, price, shipping, returns, and reviews belong right next to the buy button. Never confuse product filters with lead-form friction.

## 7. Ironclad Truth & No-Fabrication Rule

- **Never invent** a review, rating, count, price, percentage, timeframe, guarantee, "free", or "no obligation" that is not stated by the business.
- **Explanation may be illustrative; evidence never is.** Sample quote inputs or product UI demos show how a service works; customer names, numbers, badges, and results must be 100% real.
- **Never claim absent what is present.** Search the full desktop + mobile text and loaded widgets first. If an element exists (price, reviews, CTA), critique its *placement or weakness*: never call it missing.
- **Never promise a percentage conversion lift.** Explain what stops the buyer and why the fix removes that blocker.
