# Above the Fold & Ad-to-Page Message Match

80–90% of conversion weight sits on the first screen. Every paid click sees it; roughly 60% never scroll past it. And ~75% of paid-search clicks land on a phone first.

## 1. The 5-Second Cold-Read Test

Hide everything below the fold. Read ONLY the header, H1, subheadline, proof line, primary button, and hero visual as a stranger who just clicked a Google Ad. In 5 seconds they must know:

1. **What this is** (in the exact words they searched for).
2. **What outcome they get** (not just a category label).
3. **Why to trust this business** (one verifiable proof beside the action).
4. **What to press right now — and what happens next.**

If any of those four requires scrolling, the first screen is leaking paid budget.

## 2. Headline Craft (Beat Generic Category Labels)

A headline carries **one clear promise** in 6–10 words, in normal sentence case. It must include at least one of: the specific outcome the buyer wants, a concrete fact (number, time, place, spec), or the buyer's problem in their words.

- **Bad (category label):** *"Meeting Room AV for Sydney Offices"*
- **Good (outcome + specificity):** *"Walk in and start the meeting in one tap — commercial AV installed across Sydney"*
- **Bad (slogan with zero fact):** *"The Nation's Most Trusted Roofing Contractor"*
- **Good (concrete outcome + spec):** *"Metal and asphalt roofs built for hail and winds up to 120 mph"*
- **Bad (focuses on buyer's chore):** *"You answer a few questions. An accountant files."*
- **Good (leads with relief):** *"Your Self Assessment, done by a real UK accountant"*
- **Bad (AI paired fragments / riddle):** *"Five tabs, or one trip."* / *"Premium service. Local heart."*
- **Good (plain human English):** *"Organise the whole hen weekend in one shared link — reserve for £49"*

**Rules that save hours:**
- **Never stack 3 claims into one headline.** Put the main promise in the H1, put supporting specs/service range in the subhead (1–2 short lines), and put the rating/count in the proof line beside the button.
- **Preserve multi-service scope.** If the ad group or page sells 3+ core services, keep them visible in the hero/subhead — never quietly shrink a two-service business into one.
- **Subheadline never repeats the headline.** If the H1 states the outcome, the subhead explains how/for whom/what is removed (waiting, risk, disruption).
- **Keep first-screen copy under ~80 words** so the hero breathes instead of looking like an over-stuffed billboard.

## 3. Ad-to-Landing-Page Message Match

Check continuity across four seams between the Google Ad / search term and the landing page first screen:

| Seam | What breaks conversion | How to fix it |
|---|---|---|
| **Keyword / Problem scent** | Searcher types *"emergency boiler repair"*, lands on *"Welcome to Smith Heating & Plumbing"* | Mirror the searched service verbatim in the H1 or opening line of the subhead |
| **Offer continuity** | Ad says *"$0 down financing"* or *"Free same-day quote"*, landing page hides it 6 sections down (or omits it) | Put the ad's exact offer verbatim in the first screen (only if the business still offers it) |
| **Action continuity** | Ad promises *"Get instant price"*, page shows *"Submit an enquiry and we'll call you back"* | Match the button label and next-step expectation to what the ad promised |
| **Location scent** | Searcher types *"dentist in Bristol"*, hero never confirms Bristol | State the city/region plainly in the subhead or proof strip |

## 4. Hero Visual & Photo Rules

- **Lead with their best real photo, big.** Real team in front of real vans, real finished work, real product in use, or real practitioner beats every abstract gradient, stock handshake, or AI illustration.
- **Keep the subject unobstructed.** Never cover a face, van logo, roof, or product focal point with a giant text box, dark mud overlay, or floating badge soup.
- **Never stretch photos or use images with baked-in marketing text** as full-bleed hero backgrounds.
- **If the business truly has zero usable photos**, build the hero from confident brand typography, colour, and a real interactive/illustrative product moment (like a quote estimator or booking preview) — never fake stock people.

## 5. Mobile-First (390px) First-Screen Rules

Always inspect the 390px phone screen *before* desktop:

- **One-screen check:** Headline, subhead, primary CTA (or tap-to-call), and proof line must fit cleanly without pushing the action below a giant navbar, cookie banner, or oversized chat bubble.
- **No duplicate primary buttons** stacked on screen one (a sticky header button + an identical hero button 120px below it wastes precious mobile height — keep the header to logo + phone link or slim utility action if the hero holds the main CTA).
- **Slim sticky mobile action bar:** Once the hero scrolls away on a phone, keep a slim, unobtrusive sticky bar at the bottom with the exact primary action (e.g., `Call` + `Get Free Estimate`). Never let a live-chat popup swallow the bottom 25% of the screen.
- **Tap-to-call hygiene:** Every displayed phone number must be a working `tel:` link. Never show two conflicting numbers between the header, hero, and footer without flagging it.
