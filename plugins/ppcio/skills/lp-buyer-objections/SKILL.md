# Buyer Objections & Hesitation Fix

Find the exact unanswered question that stops a suitable buyer from acting — and close it right beside the decision point.

## Start here

- If a local `landing-pages/<site>/` folder exists, read `audit/buyers.json`, `audit/journey-*.json`, `audit/trust.json`, `facts.md`, and `inputs/site-page-text.md` first. Otherwise work from the pasted copy, URL, or screenshots.
- Reference `../landing-page/references/offer-and-risk-reversal.md` and `../landing-page/references/cro-doctrine.md` for the 5 depths of trust and risk-reversal placement.

## Method

1. Pick a realistic good-fit buyer and pinpoint the exact moment on the page where they hesitate. (When using simulated buyer journeys, describe them honestly as modelled reactions, never as real customer interviews.)
2. Name the core unanswered question: **price / total cost**, **risk / what if it goes wrong**, **suitability ("is this for my exact situation?")**, **effort / disruption**, **credibility**, or **what happens after I click**.
3. Quote the exact section or missing gap that leaves that doubt open.
4. Recommend the smallest high-leverage fix placed directly beside that decision point:
   - Use verified proof, numbers, or terms already on the site first (moved next to the CTA or form).
   - If the proof or guarantee does not exist on the page yet, never invent it — name the exact single piece of proof the business should supply and where to place it.
5. Never cram every possible objection into the headline. Keep the main promise clean and place each objection-handler next to the choice where it arises.

## Output

Tie three things together cleanly (under 150 words unless asked for a full objection map):
- **Buyer doubt:** The question in plain buyer words.
- **Page gap:** The quoted line or missing proof where they stall.
- **What to change:** Exact copy or proof to place beside that decision (with zero invented claims).
