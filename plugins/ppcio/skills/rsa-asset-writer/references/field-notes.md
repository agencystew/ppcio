# Field notes: writing ads that hold up

What PPC.io learned writing ads for client accounts. Google's limits are in
[rsa-rules.md](rsa-rules.md), and `rsa_lint.py` checks them. These notes
cover what a script cannot judge. Read them at step 3, when you plan the
headlines.

## Contents
- Before you write
- The 15 headlines
- After it goes live

## Before you write

1. **Read the landing page, not only the brief.** The most expensive ad
   mistake is a promise the page does not keep: the ad says "Free Trial"
   and the page offers a demo call. Every click pays for that gap. Build
   facts.md from what the page shows, and drop any claim it does not.
   Source: PPC.io ad copy playbook and ppc.io/blog/ppc-ad-copy

2. **Brand, non-brand and competitor ads do different jobs.** A brand ad
   confirms the searcher found the right business. A non-brand ad matches
   the need and says why this business. A competitor ad offers an
   alternative and does not name the competitor. Ask which one this is
   before you write. Source: PPC.io ad copy playbook

3. **Write for the common need, not the rare one.** In one teardown, a
   cleaning company's ad spoke only to "death cleanup services", which few
   people search for. The ad below it said "same day cleaning" and gave a
   price, and that is the one I would click. Lead with what most searchers
   in the ad group want. Source: ppc.io/blog/ppc-ad-copy

## The 15 headlines

4. **Filler headlines carry nothing.** "We help you reach your goals",
   "We're here to make your life easy", "Quality service", "Professional
   team". Swap each one for a fact from facts.md: a rating, years in
   business, a response time, a price. Source: ppc.io/blog/ppc-ad-copy

5. **The swap test.** If two headlines can change places and the meaning
   stays the same, one of them is wasted. "Best CRM Software", "Top CRM
   Platform" and "Leading CRM Solution" are one headline three times.
   Source: PPC.io ad copy playbook

6. **The mix that works for our clients:** headlines with the keyword,
   headlines on the problem the business solves, proof (reviews, years,
   numbers from facts.md), and a clear next step. Google's system then
   picks the best mix for each search. Source: ppc.io/blog/ppc-ad-copy

7. **Urgency needs a real end date.** "Limited time" and "Act now" with no
   deadline on the page read as a trick and cost trust. Use urgency only
   when facts.md has an offer with a date. Source: PPC.io ad copy playbook

8. **Pin for a reason you can write down.** A legal line, a regulated
   claim, or test data that shows the pinned version wins. A pin for taste
   cuts the combinations Google can test. Source: PPC.io ad copy playbook

## After it goes live

9. **One strong ad usually beats three weak ones.** Google allows up to 3
   enabled responsive search ads per ad group, and each one splits the
   data. Suggest one ad, plus a second only when there is a real test.
   Source: PPC.io ad copy playbook

10. **Do not judge asset labels too early.** A "Low" label on a new ad can
    mean too little data. Keep an asset until the ad has real volume, then
    replace the ones still rated low. Source: PPC.io ad copy playbook
