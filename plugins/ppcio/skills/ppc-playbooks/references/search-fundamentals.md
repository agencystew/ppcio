# Search Campaign Fundamentals Knowledge Foundation

> Search campaigns are the backbone of Google Ads because they intercept people at the moment of intent. Someone types a query, and your ad appears when it matches. No other advertising channel offers this level of intent precision. Every dollar in Google Ads ultimately depends on three things: choosing the right keywords, writing ads that earn the click, and sending that click to a page that converts. Everything else -- bidding, budgets, extensions, audiences -- is optimization on top of this foundation.

## First Principles

**Keywords are the atomic unit of paid search.** Get the keywords right and everything downstream -- ads, landing pages, bids, budgets -- has a fighting chance. Get them wrong and nothing else matters. A keyword is a hypothesis: "people who search this term are likely to become customers." Every keyword in an account should be testable against that hypothesis with conversion data. Keywords that generate clicks but never convert are failed hypotheses consuming budget. Keywords with strong conversion rates at profitable CPAs are validated hypotheses that deserve more investment.

**The alignment chain determines everything.** Search term to keyword to ad copy to landing page to offer. At every link, the searcher's intent must be matched by what they see next. A person searching "emergency plumber near me" who sees an ad for "general home services" and lands on a page listing 20 service categories has experienced alignment failure at every link. Fix the alignment chain before optimizing anything else. Most performance problems in Search campaigns trace back to a broken link in this chain, not to bidding, budget, or audience issues.

**Intent classification is the highest-leverage keyword decision.** The search intent ladder has four rungs. Transactional intent (buy, order, hire, book, get a quote) is the highest value because the searcher is ready to act. Commercial investigation (best, compare, versus, reviews, top alternatives) is high value because the searcher is actively evaluating options. Navigational intent (brand terms, brand plus product) is valuable when competitors bid on your brand. Informational intent (how to, what is, guide, tips, tutorial) is the lowest PPC value because the searcher wants knowledge, not a transaction. Budget allocation should follow intent value: transactional keywords get the most budget, informational keywords get the least (or none -- redirect to content marketing instead).

**Match types control the breadth of the hypothesis.** Exact match says "show my ad for this query and close variants." Phrase match says "show my ad for queries that include this meaning." Broad match says "show my ad for queries the algorithm thinks are related." Each step broadens the hypothesis, which increases reach but also increases the risk of irrelevant traffic. Broad match without Smart Bidding and negative keywords is a donation to Google. Broad match WITH Smart Bidding and a robust negative keyword strategy is the most powerful discovery mechanism in Google Ads. The match type decision is a risk-reward tradeoff that depends on conversion volume, budget, and how well you can monitor and exclude bad queries.

**Quality Score is a tax or a discount on every click.** Quality Score (1-10) has three components: expected click-through rate, ad relevance, and landing page experience. Improving Quality Score from 5 to 7 reduces actual CPC by roughly 28% without changing your bid. A Quality Score of 3 inflates CPC by roughly 40%. This means two advertisers targeting the same keyword with the same bid pay dramatically different prices. Quality Score is not a vanity metric. It is a multiplier that affects the profitability of every click. The components tell you where to invest: low expected CTR means your ads need better headlines, low ad relevance means keyword-ad alignment is broken, and low landing page experience means the page itself needs work.

## The Decision Landscape

**Keyword discovery approach by account maturity.** New accounts with no advertising history should start with transactional keywords in phrase match, conservative budgets, and aggressive negative keyword lists. This minimizes waste during the learning period while capturing the highest-value traffic. Accounts with existing organic data (Google Search Console) should bridge SEO keywords into PPC: queries already generating organic impressions have proven demand, and adding paid ads creates dual SERP presence that increases total clicks by 25-50% versus organic alone. Accounts with existing PPC data should mine search term reports for converting queries not yet added as keywords, identify coverage gaps across their product or service offering, and detect cannibalization where multiple ad groups compete for the same query.

**How many keywords are enough.** 50 well-chosen keywords with strong landing pages outperform 500 generic keywords with weak pages. The trap is equating keyword count with coverage. Each keyword needs a conversion path: a landing page with a clear offer, a relevant ad, and a realistic CPA given the expected CPC and conversion rate. A keyword without a landing page is a budget donation. Focus on keywords where you have a complete conversion path, then expand as you build new landing pages and validate new keyword hypotheses.

**Brand versus non-brand separation.** Brand keywords (your company name and branded products) convert at 10-30x the rate of non-brand keywords and at a fraction of the CPC. If brand and non-brand exist in the same campaign, the blended metrics look acceptable while non-brand waste hides behind brand efficiency. Separation is non-negotiable. Brand campaigns use Target Impression Share at 90-95% to defend owned traffic. Non-brand campaigns use Target CPA or ROAS based on actual business economics. This separation is the single most impactful structural decision in Search campaign management.

**RSA construction and testing.** Responsive Search Ads combine your headlines (up to 15) and descriptions (up to 4) dynamically. The common mistake is writing 15 near-identical headlines that rearrange the same message. Effective RSA testing requires genuine diversity: different value propositions, different proof points, different CTAs, and different emotional appeals. Each headline should communicate something unique and make sense standalone, because Google may show any combination. Pin your brand or primary keyword to Headline 1 position and your strongest value proposition to Headline 2 to ensure consistency, while letting Headline 3 and beyond rotate for testing.

**Ad extensions as competitive necessity.** Extensions (sitelinks, callouts, structured snippets, call extensions) increase ad real estate in the SERP. Accounts with full extension coverage see 10-20% higher CTR than those without. Sitelinks are the highest-impact extension, driving 10-20% of total ad clicks. Every sitelink must have both description lines filled (losing 30% of desktop CTR impact without them), point to a unique URL, and use specific, action-oriented text. "Get Free Quote" outperforms "Learn More" because it communicates both the action and the value proposition.

**Dynamic Search Ads as a discovery engine.** DSA campaigns auto-generate ads from your website content, matching page titles and content to search queries your keyword list misses. DSA is not a primary driver -- it is a discovery tool. Its highest value is not direct conversions but finding new keywords: converting search terms from DSA should be promoted to keyword campaigns as exact match. Without exclusion scaffolding (blog pages, about pages, support pages excluded), DSA sends paid traffic to non-converting content. With proper exclusions and weekly query mining, DSA captures 10-25% incremental query coverage.

## Industry Branches

### Ecommerce

Ecommerce search campaigns complement Shopping campaigns by capturing queries that do not trigger Shopping ads: branded searches with service intent ("Nike return policy"), long-tail product queries, and comparison queries. The standard ecommerce search structure separates brand, category-level product search (non-brand), DSA for long-tail discovery, and remarketing search lists for audiences (RLSA). Category campaigns should mirror the website's product taxonomy so that ad groups map directly to category landing pages.

Keyword strategy for ecommerce should emphasize product-specific terms over generic category terms. "Men's black Nike Air Max 90 size 11" converts at 3-5x the rate of "running shoes" because specificity signals intent. Long-tail product keywords have lower CPCs and higher conversion rates, making them the most profitable keyword type in ecommerce. Broad match on generic product terms ("shoes," "electronics") burns budget fast without Smart Bidding and aggressive negatives.

### Lead Generation

Lead gen search campaigns live or die on intent segmentation. High-intent keywords (get a quote, hire, book, schedule consultation) should receive 40-50% of budget because they convert at the highest rate. Commercial investigation keywords (best, top, compare, reviews) receive 25-30% because searchers are actively evaluating. Competitor keywords receive 5-10% with controlled bidding because the traffic is pre-committed to a competitor and converts at lower rates. Informational keywords should generally be excluded from PPC and redirected to content marketing.

The critical insight for lead gen is that keyword volume does not equal lead value. A high-volume keyword generating cheap leads may produce zero closed deals. A low-volume keyword generating expensive leads may produce your best customers. The only way to know is to connect search data to CRM data through offline conversion import. Without that connection, lead gen search optimization is flying blind -- optimizing for volume of something whose quality is unknown.

Ad copy for lead gen should pre-qualify. "Enterprise CRM for Teams of 50+" filters out small businesses before the click. "Starting at $500/month" filters price-sensitive prospects. Pre-qualification in ad copy increases CPA (fewer clicks) but improves lead quality (better clicks), which is almost always the profitable tradeoff when cost per lead is $50+.

### B2B SaaS

B2B SaaS search is characterized by low volume, high CPCs, and long sales cycles. Most B2B SaaS keywords generate 100-500 searches per month, meaning each click matters disproportionately. Focus on use-case queries ("[solution] for [industry]" or "[solution] for [role]") because these demonstrate both awareness and relevance. Product category keywords are broader and more competitive. Competitor keywords capture switchers but convert at lower rates.

Landing pages must match keyword specificity. A search for "CRM for real estate agents" landing on a generic "CRM for everyone" page breaks the alignment chain. Industry-specific landing pages, even variants of the same core page with adjusted messaging, improve Quality Score and conversion rate enough to pay for themselves within the first month.

The funnel gap: a demo request today may not become revenue for 60-120 days. Evaluate search performance on 90-day windows minimum, with offline conversion data identifying which keywords produce pipeline, not just demo requests.

### Local Services

Local service search operates within geographic constraints that define both opportunity and ceiling. The demand ceiling (total addressable searches in the service area) should be calculated before setting expectations. An account at 80% impression share in a limited market cannot grow through keyword optimization alone.

Front-load service-specific terms with geographic modifiers: "emergency plumber [city]," "24-hour electrician [neighborhood]." Call extensions are critical because local customers want to call, not fill out forms. Call-only campaigns often outperform standard text ads for HVAC, locksmith, and urgent medical services. Set minimum call duration at 60 seconds to filter accidental dials.

Ad scheduling matters more here than any other vertical. Running ads outside business hours generates calls to voicemail, which local customers abandon 80%+ of the time. Schedule ads to business hours only, or invest in after-hours answering before running 24/7 ads.

### High-Value Verticals (Legal, Medical, Financial)

High-CPC verticals require the tightest keyword management because each click costs $50-200+. At these CPCs, a single poorly performing ad group can consume thousands per month on irrelevant traffic. Exact match on transactional keywords is the default starting position: "[city] personal injury lawyer," "mesothelioma attorney free consultation," "wealth management advisor $1M+." Phrase match is added cautiously after reviewing search term reports. Broad match is introduced only after 3+ months of negative keyword data has been accumulated.

Negative keyword management in high-CPC verticals is not a weekly maintenance task -- it is a daily survival requirement. At $150 per click, 10 irrelevant clicks per week cost $78,000 per year. Universal negatives (jobs, salary, careers, free, DIY, pro bono, law school, medical school) must be in place before the first ad serves. Shared negative keyword lists applied across all campaigns prevent the "forgot to add negatives to the new campaign" problem that bleeds budget in the first week.

Ad copy in regulated verticals must balance compliance with conversion. Legal ads cannot guarantee outcomes. Medical ads face restrictions on treatment claims. Financial ads require disclosures. The constraint is real, but within it, specific claims outperform generic ones. "23 Years Personal Injury Experience" is both compliant and compelling. "Leading Law Firm" is compliant but meaningless. Proof points (years of experience, case results where permitted, client count, awards) do the selling within regulatory bounds.

## Diagnostic Trees

**IF CTR is below 3% on high-intent keywords:** The ad copy is not matching search intent. Check whether the primary keyword appears in at least one headline. Verify the ad communicates a clear value proposition beyond just repeating the keyword. Review whether competitors' ads offer stronger proof points or more specific CTAs. A CTR below 3% on transactional keywords usually means the ad is generic while competitors are specific.

**IF CPA is above target but CTR is healthy:** The landing page is the bottleneck. The ad is earning clicks (CTR is fine), but the page is not converting them. Check: does the landing page headline match the ad headline? Is there a clear, single CTA above the fold? Does the page load in under 3 seconds on mobile? Is the form asking for too many fields? Landing page conversion rate below 3% on high-intent traffic is a red flag that the page, not the campaign, needs fixing.

**IF a keyword has high impressions, high clicks, but zero conversions over 30 days:** This keyword's hypothesis has failed. It is attracting clicks from people who do not convert. Before pausing, check: is the landing page appropriate for this keyword's intent? Is the keyword matching search terms you did not expect (check the search term report)? Is the conversion tracking working on this landing page? If alignment and tracking are confirmed, the keyword is a bad fit for the business and should be paused or moved to observation at reduced bids.

**IF Quality Score components show "Below Average" on landing page experience:** Google is telling you the landing page is the weakest link. Check mobile experience first (Google weights mobile heavily). Then page speed (above 3 seconds is penalized). Then content relevance (does the page content match the keyword?). Landing page experience improvements lift Quality Score for every keyword pointing to that page, producing account-wide CPC reductions.

**IF search terms report shows more than 20% irrelevant queries by spend:** The negative keyword architecture is insufficient. Add negatives in layers: universal waste terms (jobs, free, DIY, reddit) at the account level, brand terms as negatives on non-brand campaigns, cross-campaign negatives to prevent keyword cannibalization, and ad-group-level negatives for intent sculpting. After adding negatives, verify the additions did not block legitimate traffic by checking impression volume over the following week.

## Mental Models

**The Intent Ladder.** Every keyword sits on a rung of the intent ladder: informational (learning), commercial investigation (evaluating), navigational (seeking a specific brand), transactional (ready to buy). Budget flows down from the top rung. Transactional keywords get funded first because they convert at the highest rate. Commercial investigation second. Navigational (brand) gets protected. Informational gets redirected to content marketing. Moving budget upward on the ladder (from transactional to informational) without exhausting the lower rungs first is a common mistake. The model breaks down when transactional volume is too small to absorb available budget, in which case commercial investigation keywords become the primary investment.

**The SEO-PPC Halo.** Pages ranking in the top 3 organically for a keyword typically see 15-25% higher Quality Scores when that keyword is used in Google Ads. The organic ranking signals to Google that the page is relevant and high-quality, which translates into higher expected CTR and better landing page experience scores. This means PPC and SEO are not separate channels competing for budget. They are complementary forces that reduce each other's costs. A keyword where you rank organically top 3 AND run a paid ad captures more total clicks (25-50% more) than either channel alone.

**The Negative Keyword Blueprint.** Think of your keyword strategy as two halves: what you target and what you exclude. The exclusion half is equally important because every irrelevant click at $10+ CPC directly destroys profitability. Four layers of negatives form the blueprint: universal waste (jobs, free, DIY, reddit), brand isolation (brand terms as negatives on non-brand campaigns), cross-campaign sculpting (exact-match negatives preventing campaigns from competing with each other), and intent sculpting (ad-group-level negatives routing queries to the most relevant ad group). This architecture should be designed before campaigns launch, not added reactively.

**The Keyword Lifecycle.** Keywords move through phases: hypothesis (newly added, unproven), testing (accumulating data, 50-100 clicks), validated (converting profitably), scaling (increasing bids and budget), and mature (stable performance, monitor for decay). Each phase requires different management: hypotheses need patience, testing keywords need search term monitoring, validated keywords need bid optimization, scaling keywords need budget headroom, and mature keywords need decay detection. Treating all keywords with the same management cadence ignores the fact that they are at different stages of proof.

**The Landing Page Leverage Point.** Improving landing page conversion rate from 3% to 5% has the same revenue impact as reducing CPC by 40%, but it applies to every keyword pointing to that page simultaneously. A conversion rate improvement is a permanent multiplier, while a CPC reduction often reverts as competition changes. This model reframes where optimization effort should go: landing pages first (multiplicative, persistent), then keywords (additive, per-keyword), then bids (temporary, market-dependent).

## Thresholds and Benchmarks

**5-15 keywords per ad group** is the target range. Below 5 suggests over-segmentation that will starve the ad group of impression volume. Above 20 suggests mixed intent that will produce irrelevant ad-to-keyword matches. This range comes from the practical observation that 5-15 keywords can share a landing page, share ad copy, and represent a coherent intent cluster.

**1,000 impressions/month per ad group** is the minimum for statistically meaningful data. Below this, the ad group cannot generate enough clicks for conversion analysis. Consolidate ad groups below this threshold into broader groups, or verify that the keyword set is valid and increase bids to gain impression share.

**30 characters per headline, 90 characters per description** are the RSA character limits. Headlines exceeding 30 characters get rejected. Descriptions exceeding 90 get truncated. Check character counts before deploying any ad copy. Title tags from SEO pages often exceed 30 characters and must be truncated or rewritten for use as headlines.

**3% CTR benchmark for transactional keywords.** Below 3% on high-intent keywords signals an ad relevance problem. Industry averages vary (legal and B2B often see 4-6%, ecommerce 2-3%), but 3% is the general threshold below which ad copy should be investigated. Brand keywords typically achieve 8-15% CTR and should be evaluated separately.

**3-5% landing page conversion rate** is the benchmark for high-intent traffic. Pages below 3% on transactional traffic have a conversion mechanism problem (confusing layout, weak CTA, too many form fields, slow load speed). Pages above 5% are performing well and should be protected. Compare pages receiving similar traffic quality before concluding one is underperforming -- a page receiving informational traffic at 2% conversion rate may be fine, while a page receiving transactional traffic at 2% is underperforming.

**Weekly search term review** is the management cadence for the first 4-6 weeks of any campaign, reducing to biweekly after the negative keyword architecture is established. At $10+ CPCs, even 3-5 days of irrelevant traffic can cost hundreds. The review should produce three outputs: negatives to add, converting search terms to promote to keywords, and alignment insights that inform ad copy and landing page improvements.

**15-25% Quality Score improvement** is the typical gain from strong organic presence on the same keywords. This is the SEO-PPC halo effect quantified. Keywords where you rank organically top 3 tend to receive Quality Scores 1-2 points higher than keywords where you have no organic presence, translating to 15-25% lower CPCs.

## Common Mistakes and WHY They Happen

**Starting with broad match on a new account.** Google defaults to broad match in the setup wizard. For established accounts with Smart Bidding and conversion data, broad match is powerful. For new accounts with zero conversion history, it generates clicks the algorithm cannot evaluate for quality. Start with phrase match, build conversion data, then expand to broad match after the algorithm has a training set.

**Writing the same headline 15 different ways.** "Best CRM Software," "Top CRM Platform," and "Leading CRM Solution" are the same headline wearing different outfits. If all 15 headlines say the same thing, there is nothing to test. Use a structured framework (keyword, benefit, proof, CTA, differentiator angles) to ensure each headline communicates a distinct message.

**Optimizing keywords without checking landing pages.** When CPA rises, the instinct is to adjust bids or add negatives. But the most common root cause is landing page degradation: a broken form, slow load, or redesign that removed the CTA from above the fold. Check the landing page before touching the campaign.

**Running ads 24/7 when the business cannot respond 24/7.** At $50+ CPCs, four unanswered after-hours calls per night cost $6,000/month. Ad scheduling to business hours is not optional for call-based businesses.

**Treating all keywords with the same management cadence.** A brand keyword at $5 CPA and a non-brand keyword at $75 CPA require fundamentally different strategies. Managing them in the same campaign produces a blended metric that masks true economics. This mistake is invisible in aggregate reporting.

## What Changes Over Time

Match types continue to broaden. Exact match now includes close variants, plural forms, and implied intent matches. Phrase match captures semantic meaning beyond word order. This trend reduces the precision of manual keyword control and increases reliance on negative keywords and Smart Bidding to filter intent. Accounts built on exact-match precision must adapt by investing more in negative keyword management and conversion tracking quality.

Google's AI-powered features (AI Max for Search, automatically created assets, broad match recommendations) push accounts toward automation. The strategic response is not resistance but governance: use automation where data supports it, maintain manual control where data is thin, and always verify that automated recommendations align with business economics rather than Google's revenue goals.

Extensions are being rebranded as "assets" and integrated more deeply into the ad creation flow. The distinction between ads and extensions is blurring as Google assembles ad units dynamically from all available assets. This means asset completeness (sitelinks, callouts, structured snippets, images) becomes a competitive baseline, not an optimization advantage. Accounts without full asset coverage are structurally disadvantaged in ad auctions.

First-party data (customer lists, offline conversion imports, consent-mode signals) is becoming more important as third-party cookies phase out and privacy regulations tighten. Search campaigns that incorporate first-party data through customer match, offline conversion import, and enhanced conversions will have bidding and targeting advantages over campaigns that rely solely on Google's in-session data.

## Connected Domains

**Account structure** determines how keywords are organized and whether data pools are deep enough for Smart Bidding. Keyword strategy and account structure are two sides of the same coin.

**Negative keywords** are the defensive half of keyword strategy. Without a four-layer negative architecture, even well-chosen positive keywords waste budget on irrelevant search terms.

**Bidding strategy** determines what you pay for each click. Keyword volume determines which bid strategies are viable, and bid strategies determine whether keyword expansion is profitable.

**Landing pages** are the conversion endpoint. The alignment chain runs from keyword to ad to landing page, and the weakest link determines conversion rate. Landing page optimization often produces larger gains than keyword or bid changes.

**Shopping campaigns** complement Search for ecommerce by capturing product-specific queries. Account for Shopping coverage to avoid bidding against yourself.

**Performance Max** captures some search queries through automated targeting. Monitor Search impression share after PMax launch to detect cannibalization.
