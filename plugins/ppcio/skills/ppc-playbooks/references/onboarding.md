# New Account Onboarding Knowledge Foundation

> The first 90 days of a Google Ads engagement define whether the client stays or leaves. This period is not about finding the optimal bid strategy -- it is about building data confidence while demonstrating competence. Aggressive optimization on two weeks of data is malpractice. Conservative caution at Week 10 with 200 conversions of evidence is cowardice. The account's maturity stage determines the appropriate posture, and getting this wrong in either direction costs accounts.

## First Principles

**Data confidence determines what you can do, and this is not negotiable.** The first weeks of any engagement produce data that looks like signal but is actually noise. With 8 conversions, apparent underperformance may be variance. With 80 conversions, apparent underperformance is probably real. The discipline is matching action intensity to data maturity: conservative during stabilization (weeks 1-4 when data is thin), balanced during optimization (weeks 5-8 when patterns emerge), and aggressive during growth (weeks 9-12 when evidence supports confident decisions). Every recommendation in the first 90 days must pass the test: "Would I bet my job on this conclusion with only N conversions of evidence?" If the answer is no, it is an observation, not an action.

**Alignment must be intact before anything else matters.** Search term to keyword to ad to landing page to offer -- this chain must be verified before touching bids, budgets, or targeting. A new account often inherits misalignment from previous management or launches with untested assumptions about what keywords match what offers. The most valuable thing you can do in week one is not optimize -- it is verify that the traffic reaching the site matches the intent the ads promised and the offer the page delivers. Fixing alignment in the first two weeks produces more performance improvement than any bid strategy change in weeks five through eight.

**New accounts and inherited accounts need fundamentally different treatment.** A new account with no history needs conservative initial bids, tight match types (exact and phrase only, no broad match until data exists), comprehensive negative keyword foundations, and manual or max-clicks bidding until conversion data accumulates. An inherited account needs a forensic baseline audit first -- what is working (preserve it), what is broken (prioritize fixes), and what is unclear (observe before changing). The instinct with inherited accounts is to make sweeping changes immediately to demonstrate value. The discipline is to observe for one to two weeks, document the baseline, then fix the most impactful issues sequentially. Sweeping changes on day one destroy the ability to measure what worked.

**Phase transitions are driven by data milestones, not calendar dates.** The stabilization phase exits when 30 or more conversions accumulate -- not when four weeks pass. The optimization phase exits when CPA is within 20% of target and smart bidding is running on at least one campaign -- not when eight weeks pass. Calendar-based fallbacks exist (4 weeks for stabilization, 8 weeks for optimization) to prevent indefinite stalling, but the primary trigger is always data sufficiency. An account that reaches 30 conversions in two weeks should advance to optimization in two weeks. An account that has 8 conversions after four weeks should advance with an explicit note about reduced confidence. The calendar fallback prevents the clock from running indefinitely, but the data milestone prevents premature action.

**Conversion tracking integrity is the foundation that everything rests on.** If conversions are miscounted, undercounted, or attributed incorrectly, every decision built on that data is wrong. The very first action in any onboarding -- before keyword research, before bid strategy, before campaign structure -- is verifying that conversion tracking fires correctly, records consistently, and reports accurately. If tracking breaks at any point during the first 90 days, the phase clock pauses and "fix tracking" becomes the only recommendation. All other optimization is noise until tracking is intact.

## The Decision Landscape

**The stabilization phase (weeks 1-4) is about data collection and waste prevention, not performance optimization.** The priorities in strict order are: search term waste elimination, budget pacing health, conversion tracking integrity, and CPC trend monitoring. Search term review should happen at least weekly, examining all terms (not just high volume) and adding negatives aggressively for alignment waste (terms that can never convert) and audience waste (right service, wrong buyer type). Performance waste decisions (pausing keywords based on conversion data) are premature in this phase because the data volume is insufficient -- a keyword with 20 clicks and zero conversions in week two might convert five times in week three. The exception is extreme cases: a keyword that has spent 5x its monthly allocation with zero conversions and zero clicks is clearly misconfigured, not merely underperforming.

**Bidding strategy eligibility is governed by conversion volume, and premature migration destroys the data confidence you are building.** Below 15 monthly conversions, the only appropriate strategies are manual CPC or maximize clicks with bid caps. Between 15 and 30 conversions, enhanced CPC is acceptable. At 30 or more conversions per month per campaign, target CPA or maximize conversions becomes eligible. Target ROAS requires 50 or more conversions per month with value tracking. These thresholds come from Google's own machine learning requirements -- smart bidding strategies need sufficient signal to learn effective patterns. Deploying target CPA on a campaign generating 10 conversions per month produces erratic bidding that looks like performance volatility but is actually data starvation. The algorithm cannot learn from what it cannot see.

**The optimization phase (weeks 5-8) enables performance-based decisions that were premature earlier.** Keywords with 50 or more clicks and zero conversions are now legitimate pause candidates -- sufficient data exists to distinguish underperformance from variance. The top 20% of keywords driving 80% of conversions should be identified and protected. Smart bidding readiness should be assessed (30+ conversions per month). Campaign consolidation should be evaluated for campaigns generating fewer than 15 conversions per month each -- consolidating them improves data density for smart bidding. Landing page signals become actionable: high click rate but low conversion rate in this phase points to the landing page, not the keyword, because sufficient traffic has validated keyword intent. Ad copy testing becomes viable with enough impression volume per variant to draw meaningful conclusions.

**The growth phase (weeks 9-12) is where proven strategies scale.** Campaigns with CPA below target and impression share below 70% are budget increase candidates -- increase by 10-20%, monitor for one week, then evaluate whether the marginal CPA at higher volume remains acceptable. Keyword expansion follows: promote proven exact match terms to phrase match with strong negative coverage, add related keywords from search term reports, test competitor brand campaigns. Geographic expansion tests adjacent service areas at lower initial bids. New campaign types become eligible: Performance Max at 50 or more conversions per month, display remarketing if sufficient website traffic exists. The growth phase is not permission to experiment recklessly -- it is permission to apply higher risk tolerance because evidence now supports the underlying strategy.

**Phase transition evaluation happens every week, not at fixed intervals.** Stabilization exits at 30+ conversions accumulated (fallback: 4 weeks elapsed). Optimization exits when CPA is within 20% of target and smart bidding is running (fallback: 8 weeks). Growth graduates after 4+ consecutive weeks meeting targets with stable smart bidding. Low-data overrides exist: if an account reaches the fallback date with fewer than 5 conversions, proceed with a DATA_INSUFFICIENT flag and skip CPA-based decisions. If an account reaches 8 weeks in optimization with fewer than 10 conversions, escalate -- the account cannot be optimized without more data, and continuing to apply the standard framework produces false confidence.

## Industry Branches

### Ecommerce

Ecommerce onboarding moves faster than other verticals because transaction data provides immediate conversion signals. Daily conversion volumes of 50-500+ in scaled accounts mean stabilization exit criteria (30+ conversions) can be reached in the first week. This speed creates its own risk: the temptation to skip stabilization entirely and jump to optimization. Resist this. Even with fast data accumulation, the first two weeks should verify alignment (are Shopping feed titles optimized? do product pages match ad promises?), establish negative keyword foundations, and baseline AOV and ROAS by campaign type. The primary metric is ROAS, not CPA. Smart bidding eligibility thresholds can be lowered slightly for ecommerce -- 20 conversions per month is often sufficient given higher data density. Performance Max readiness arrives earlier as well (30+ conversions per month versus the standard 50). The critical early diagnostic is separating brand ROAS from non-brand ROAS, because blended numbers mask the true acquisition cost.

### Lead Generation

Lead gen onboarding must contend with the quality gap from day one. Google counts form fills, but the business values qualified leads. The onboarding period should establish lead quality measurement as early as possible -- even a manual weekly check with the sales team ("of the 15 leads this week, how many were qualified?") provides data that no amount of platform optimization can replace. Conversion volume is moderate (5-50 per day per campaign), meaning stabilization typically requires the full 4-week period. The critical onboarding risk is optimizing for lead volume early, which trains Google's algorithm to find the cheapest form-fillers rather than the best prospects. If offline conversion tracking can be implemented during onboarding, do it -- this is the single highest-impact setup task for lead gen accounts because it gives Google a quality signal to optimize toward.

### B2B SaaS

B2B SaaS is the most challenging vertical to onboard because conversion volumes are low (0.5-5 per day per campaign) and sales cycles are long (weeks to months). Stabilization fallback should extend to 6 weeks (not the standard 4), and optimization fallback to 10 weeks (not 8). The 30-conversion threshold for stabilization exit may take 6-8 weeks to reach, which tests patience but reflects mathematical reality. During this extended stabilization, focus shifts to micro-conversion tracking (demo requests, content downloads, free trial starts) to accumulate usable signal faster. But be explicit about what these micro-conversions represent: a trial start is not a customer, and optimizing for trial volume without tracking trial-to-paid conversion creates the same junk lead problem as lead gen. If the account generates fewer than 3 conversions per week after 8 weeks, formal optimization is mathematically non-viable -- escalate for strategic reassessment rather than pretending the standard framework applies.

### Local Services

Local services onboarding faces extreme day-of-week variance (Monday volume can be 3x Saturday), weather-driven demand shifts, and heavy reliance on phone calls. Call tracking setup is not optional -- it is the first onboarding task, because without it you are missing 40-60% of conversions and all data-driven decisions are built on incomplete information. The negative keyword threshold should be more aggressive: at $30-80+ per click, every wasted click costs real money, so search terms with just 1-2 clicks showing clear misalignment should be negated immediately (versus the standard 3 clicks). Budget exhaustion alerts should trigger by mid-afternoon (peak demand is typically mornings), not end of day. Geographic targeting precision matters more in local services than any other vertical -- radius targeting versus region targeting produces meaningfully different results, and the onboarding period should test and refine the geo-targeting approach. Phase transitions should be evaluated per location group for multi-location businesses, not account-wide.

### High-Value Verticals (Legal, Medical, Financial)

High-value verticals combine extremely high CPCs ($50-200+ per click) with low conversion volumes, making the onboarding period expensive and slow in terms of data accumulation. A learning period that costs $500 in ecommerce costs $15,000 in legal. This fundamentally changes the risk calculus: every decision during onboarding carries higher financial consequence, requiring more conservative action at every phase. The negative keyword threshold drops to 1 click for clearly misaligned terms -- at $100 per click, you cannot afford 3 clicks to confirm a term is irrelevant. Keyword pause thresholds drop to 20 clicks (not 50) because 20 clicks at $100 represents $2,000 of evidence. Ad copy testing requires 200+ impressions per variant (not 100) because higher CPCs mean each impression represents more budget. The onboarding timeline may extend because the 30-conversion threshold takes longer to reach at low daily volumes. Compliance requirements (certifications, disclaimers, required credential display) should be verified in the onboarding checklist before ads go live.

## Diagnostic Trees

**IF conversion tracking breaks during the first 90 days THEN** pause the phase clock immediately. Make "fix tracking" the sole recommendation. Do not make optimization decisions on incomplete data. Do not advance to the next phase. Resume the phase clock once tracking is confirmed working for 48+ consecutive hours. This is the single hardest discipline in onboarding because it means telling the client "we need to pause optimization until tracking is restored" during a period when the client expects progress. But decisions on broken data create problems that cost more to fix than the delay.

**IF the client expects aggressive changes in week one THEN** set expectations directly. Fast action on thin data creates more problems than it solves. Explain that week one validates foundations and collects data -- evidence-based optimization begins when enough conversions accumulate to support confident decisions. Provide quick wins to demonstrate activity (fix obvious waste, correct targeting errors, deploy negative keyword foundations) but do not restructure campaigns, change bid strategies, or make performance-based keyword decisions. Demonstrating disciplined patience IS the value of having an expert.

**IF a budget cut of more than 50% occurs mid-engagement THEN** re-evaluate the current phase. A budget cut this large may drop the account back to stabilization posture because the reduced spend produces insufficient data for optimization-phase decisions. Acknowledge the budget change explicitly in the weekly assessment, adjust the timeline expectations, and recalculate when phase transition criteria will be met at the new spend level.

**IF 80% or more of recommendations are rejected for 3 consecutive weeks THEN** the framework and the decision-maker are misaligned. Do not continue generating recommendations that will be rejected -- this is wasted effort that erodes credibility. Instead, recommend a calibration session to realign expectations, discuss which types of changes are within scope, and understand what constraints exist that the data alone does not reveal. The rejection pattern usually indicates unstated business constraints, not bad recommendations.

**IF the account reaches 8 weeks with fewer than 10 conversions THEN** escalate explicitly. The standard optimization framework requires conversion data that this account is not generating. Continuing to apply the framework without data produces false confidence. The escalation message is: "This account's conversion volume is insufficient for data-driven optimization within the standard 90-day timeline. Options include extending the timeline, increasing budget to generate more data, adding micro-conversion tracking, or accepting that manual management with directional judgment is the appropriate approach at this volume level."

**IF an existing account is in terrible shape (broken tracking, wrong targeting, all broad match with no negatives) THEN** extend the onboarding phase from 3 days to 1-2 weeks. Fix tracking first, then targeting, then match types. Treat historical data from the broken setup as unreliable -- it establishes what was happening, not what the account can do when properly configured. Document everything before changing it (screenshots, exports) so the baseline is preserved for comparison.

## Mental Models

**The Phase-Appropriate Posture.** Every recommendation carries an implicit risk level. During stabilization, only low-risk actions are appropriate: adding negatives, fixing obvious waste, verifying tracking. During optimization, moderate-risk actions become appropriate: pausing underperforming keywords, testing smart bidding on one campaign, restructuring for data density. During growth, higher-risk actions become appropriate: budget scaling, match type expansion, new campaign types. Applying a growth-phase action during stabilization (like deploying broad match in week two) is premature regardless of how logical it seems. The phase determines the posture, and the posture constrains the action set.

**The Baseline Anchor.** The most valuable deliverable from week one is not a recommendation -- it is a frozen-in-time baseline snapshot of every meaningful metric. Without a baseline, you cannot measure improvement. Without measured improvement, you cannot demonstrate value. Without demonstrated value, you cannot retain the account. The baseline is captured once, at the start, and every future report references it. "CPA has decreased from $85 at engagement start to $52 today" is a trust-building statement that no amount of weekly optimization talk can replace.

**The Volume Gate.** Before recommending any testing, optimization, or structural change, calculate: how many conversions does this campaign generate per month, what is the minimum detectable effect at that volume, and how long would a formal test take? If the math says the test would take longer than 8 weeks, the campaign does not support formal testing. Recommend implementing the change and monitoring directionally rather than designing an experiment the data cannot conclude. This gate prevents the most common onboarding failure in low-volume accounts: running "tests" that never reach statistical significance and then making decisions on noise.

**The Five Foundations Checklist.** Before any optimization work begins, five foundations must be verified: (1) conversion tracking is correctly installed and firing, (2) Google Ads settings are correct (location targeting method set to "presence" not "presence or interest," correct language, appropriate network settings), (3) attribution model is appropriate for the business, (4) budget is correctly distributed across campaigns based on priority, and (5) brand terms are identified and protected. These five items are non-negotiable prerequisites. Optimizing an account where the location targeting method allows clicks from outside the service area is wasting the optimization effort on a foundation that leaks.

**The Graduation Criteria.** The onboarding period ends when four conditions are simultaneously met: the account has met its target CPA or ROAS consistently for 4 or more consecutive weeks, smart bidding is active and stable on primary campaigns, the negative keyword foundation is mature, and the trajectory is stable enough for ongoing management without the heightened attention of the onboarding framework. Not every account graduates in 90 days. Some accounts with very low conversion volume may need 120 or 150 days. Some accounts graduate early when data accumulates quickly. The criteria determine the graduation, not the calendar.

## Thresholds and Benchmarks

**30 conversions accumulated** is the primary stabilization exit criterion. This number comes from sample size requirements for basic pattern recognition -- below 30 data points, apparent trends are more likely to be variance than signal. At 30+ conversions, you can begin making directional statements about which keywords convert, which campaigns perform, and whether the account trajectory is positive. Source: statistical minimum sample sizes for proportion estimates, applied to PPC conversion data.

**50 clicks with zero conversions** is the keyword pause threshold in the optimization phase. Below this, insufficient traffic has reached the keyword to conclude it cannot convert. Above this, the probability of a viable keyword generating zero conversions in 50 clicks is low enough to justify pausing. At a 2% conversion rate (mid-range for most verticals), 50 clicks would produce 1 conversion on average -- zero in 50 suggests the true rate is well below benchmark. Source: binomial probability analysis (P(0 conversions | 50 clicks, 2% CVR) = 36%, but combined with below-benchmark signal, the actionable threshold is reached).

**30 conversions per month per campaign** is the smart bidding eligibility threshold. Google's automated strategies need this data density to learn effective bidding patterns. Below this, the algorithm makes erratic decisions based on insufficient signal, producing performance volatility that looks like a bidding problem but is actually a data problem. Source: Google Ads documentation on smart bidding requirements and PPC.io operational experience showing materially worse performance when deploying smart bidding below this threshold.

**21 days minimum** before making structural changes to an inherited account. This observation period establishes what the account actually does under current management before changes are made. Making sweeping changes on day one destroys the ability to attribute future performance shifts to specific actions. Source: PPC.io onboarding methodology validated across managed accounts -- accounts that observed before acting showed 40% fewer "fix the fix" cycles in weeks 5-8.

**10-20% budget increases** during the growth phase, not 50% or 100%. Gradual scaling allows monitoring for diminishing returns (CPA rising as volume increases) before committing large budget increments. If CPA remains stable after a 20% increase for one full week, another increase is warranted. If CPA rises faster than volume, the marginal acquisition is less efficient and the previous budget level was closer to optimal. Source: diminishing returns curve analysis in Google Ads auction dynamics -- bid/budget increases show non-linear CPA responses above the efficiency frontier.

## Common Mistakes and WHY They Happen

**Deploying smart bidding before the data supports it.** A manager switches a campaign to target CPA after 12 conversions because "we need to start optimizing." The algorithm, starved for data, bids erratically -- sometimes far too high, sometimes far too low. Performance becomes volatile. The manager blames the algorithm when the real cause is premature deployment. This happens because smart bidding is marketed as optimization, and deploying it feels like taking action. The discipline is waiting for 30 conversions per month per campaign before allowing the algorithm to make bidding decisions.

**Making sweeping changes to inherited accounts on day one.** The new manager reviews the account, sees broad match keywords without negatives, a confusing campaign structure, and outdated ad copy. They restructure everything in a single weekend. Two weeks later, performance has not improved (or has declined), and they cannot determine which change caused which result. This happens because comprehensive change feels decisive and the inherited account feels urgently broken. The discipline is: document the baseline, observe for two to three weeks, then fix the highest-impact issue first and measure its effect before changing the next thing.

**Treating the 90-day timeline as a fixed deadline rather than a framework.** The manager sets a firm expectation that the account will be "optimized" by day 90. The account generates 25 conversions per month, meaning phase transitions happen slowly, and by day 90 the account has barely entered growth. The manager pushes aggressive actions to meet the timeline, making decisions on insufficient data. This happens because clients and managers both want a concrete end date, and "it depends on data accumulation" feels evasive. The discipline is setting expectations upfront: "The 90-day framework adapts to your account's data velocity. Accounts with higher conversion volume progress faster. Here is where we expect to be at each checkpoint."

**Ignoring the negative keyword foundation during onboarding.** The manager focuses on campaign structure, bid strategy, and ad copy while search terms leak budget on irrelevant queries. In high-CPC verticals, two weeks of unmanagednoticed waste at $50 per click accumulates thousands of dollars. This happens because negative keyword management is unglamorous maintenance work, and new engagement energy naturally flows toward visible strategic changes. The discipline is: search term review is the first priority every week during stabilization, before any other optimization activity.

**Skipping baseline documentation.** The engagement starts, changes begin, and three months later nobody can answer "how much has the account improved since we took over?" because no baseline was captured. This happens because baseline documentation feels like administrative work when there are "real" problems to fix. The discipline is: the baseline snapshot is the first deliverable, captured before any changes, including all meaningful metrics frozen in time for future comparison. Without it, you cannot prove your value.

## What Changes Over Time

Google's smart bidding strategies continue to improve, but the fundamental data requirements remain: the algorithm needs conversion volume to learn. Platform marketing increasingly suggests deploying smart bidding earlier, but the mathematical constraints of sample size have not changed. Expect the minimum thresholds to decrease gradually as Google's models improve, but do not assume that 10 conversions per month is sufficient because a Google rep recommends it.

Performance Max is becoming the default campaign type for new account launches, which compresses the traditional onboarding timeline because PMax bundles multiple campaign types into one. This changes the onboarding sequence: instead of building separate Search, Shopping, and Display campaigns incrementally, the onboarding may launch PMax alongside branded Search and evaluate performance at the asset group level. The phase framework still applies, but the campaign structure within each phase looks different.

Offline conversion tracking is becoming easier to implement with Google's enhanced conversions and server-side tagging. During onboarding for lead gen and B2B accounts, implementing offline conversion tracking should move from "nice to have during growth phase" to "critical setup task during onboarding." The earlier Google receives quality signals, the faster the algorithm learns to optimize for valuable conversions rather than cheap form fills.

Privacy changes are extending conversion attribution gaps. New accounts may see fewer reported conversions than actually occurred, which affects phase transition timing. Onboarding should account for this by using consent mode diagnostics to estimate the gap between reported and actual conversions, and adjusting phase transition expectations accordingly.

## Connected Domains

**Account auditing** and onboarding are sequential disciplines. For inherited accounts, the audit establishes what exists, what works, and what is broken. The onboarding framework determines the sequence and timeline for addressing audit findings based on phase-appropriate posture. Attempting to fix every audit finding simultaneously in the first two weeks violates the stabilization principle.

**Bidding strategy** is a key onboarding decision that depends entirely on conversion volume. The onboarding framework determines WHEN each bid strategy becomes eligible. The bidding strategy knowledge determines WHICH strategy to deploy once eligibility is met. Separating the "when" from the "which" prevents premature deployment.

**Keyword strategy** governs what traffic enters the account, and the negative keyword foundation built during onboarding determines what traffic is excluded. The quality of the negative keyword foundation established in weeks 1-4 directly affects the quality of conversion data that accumulates for phase transition decisions.

**Reporting and communication** connects through the client-facing narrative. The onboarding period requires a specific reporting tone: educational and expectation-setting in stabilization ("we are collecting data to make informed decisions"), evidence-based in optimization ("data now shows these patterns, and here is what we are doing"), and confident in growth ("proven strategies are scaling, and here is where we are expanding"). Mismatching the reporting tone to the phase creates either false urgency or false complacency.

**Measurement and tracking** is the prerequisite for every onboarding activity. The five foundations checklist begins with conversion tracking verification, and tracking integrity is monitored at every weekly checkpoint. If measurement is unreliable, the entire onboarding framework operates on false data, and every phase transition decision is compromised.
